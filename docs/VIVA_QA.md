# SonicFusion 2: Technical Viva Voce & Defense Guide (Q&A)

This document compiles 20 technical questions an examiner or technical auditor could ask regarding the **SonicFusion 2** architecture, data hygiene, evaluation protocols, and empirical findings. Each answer is strictly 3–5 sentences and directly grounded in the repository's configuration, source code, metrics logs, and audit records. Where an institutional or design rationale is not recorded in the codebase, it is explicitly flagged with `[author must confirm: ...]` alongside the most probable technical justifications.

### Q1: Why is the top-1 classification accuracy only around 50%–52%, and is this considered an acceptable result?
SonicFusion 2 evaluates an 8-class balanced music genre classification problem where uniform random guessing achieves an accuracy of only 12.50% ($1/8$), as recorded in [`reports/metrics/extra_models_val.json#L18`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/extra_models_val.json#L18). The test accuracy of 50.62% for GenreCNN and 52.38% for the soft-voting ensemble represents a 4.0× to 4.2× improvement over this chance baseline ([`reports/metrics/final_test.json#L225`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/final_test.json#L225), [`L299`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/final_test.json#L299)). In published literature on the official artist-separated FMA Small benchmark, classical models and standard convolutional networks consistently plateau between 40% and 50% test accuracy due to the strict elimination of artist leakage [needs author citation]. Furthermore, musical genre boundaries are permeable rather than mutually exclusive, which is why top-3 accuracy reaches 81.38%–82.62% ([`reports/metrics/final_test.json#L227`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/final_test.json#L227), [`L301`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/final_test.json#L301)), confirming that the models extract strong, reliable acoustic signals.

### Q3: Why was FMA Small chosen instead of GTZAN or other music genre datasets?
The legacy GTZAN dataset is small (only 1,000 clips) and suffers from severe, well-documented flaws including artist and album repetition across randomly generated splits, acoustic distortion, and mislabeled tracks [needs author citation]. Conversely, massive web-scale corpora like the Million Song Dataset or AudioSet require hundreds of gigabytes of disk storage and multi-GPU clusters, which conflicts with SonicFusion 2's reproducible, self-contained desktop deployment ([`configs/config.yaml`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/configs/config.yaml)). ### Q4: How do the dataset splits and evaluation protocols work in this project?
The project strictly adopts the official FMA Small partition from `fma_metadata/tracks.csv`, allocating 6,394 clean tracks to training, 800 tracks to validation, and 800 tracks to testing ([`reports/metrics/manifest_audit.json#L12-L16`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/manifest_audit.json#L12-L16)). Validation was used for all tuning and ensemble-weight selection; reported test numbers come from `scripts/11_evaluate_final_test.py`; an earlier test run exists in `test_metrics.json`. All model weights, preprocessing pipelines, and ensemble weights were completely frozen prior to the final test evaluation ([`reports/metrics/final_test.json#L3`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/final_test.json#L3)). No hyperparameter was tuned, adjusted, or re-selected after evaluating on the test set, guaranteeing zero post-hoc evaluation leakage.

### Q5: What do Macro-F1 and Top-3 Accuracy measure, and why are they reported alongside standard accuracy?
Macro-F1 calculates the unweighted arithmetic mean of F1-scores across all 8 classes ($\frac{1}{8}\sum \text{F1}_c$), penalizing models that perform well only on easily separable genres while failing on challenging classes such as Pop or Experimental ([`reports/metrics/final_test.json#L72-L75`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/final_test.json#L72-L75)). Top-3 Accuracy measures the frequency with which the ground-truth genre is included among the top three predicted probability outputs ([`reports/metrics/final_test.json#L20`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/final_test.json#L20)). In musical taxonomy, tracks frequently blend elements of multiple styles, meaning a top-1 prediction error often represents a musically plausible secondary genre [needs author citation]. Reporting top-3 accuracy alongside Macro-F1 demonstrates that while top-1 accuracy is ~50.6%–52.4%, the true genre appears in the top-3 predictions over 81%–82.6% of the time ([`reports/metrics/final_test.json#L227`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/final_test.json#L227), [`L301`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/final_test.json#L301)).

### Q7: How was data leakage audited and prevented across feature preprocessing and model training?
An automated audit confirmed zero artist overlap across all split pairs (0 in train-val, 0 in train-test, 0 in val-test across 2,307 artists) and zero split assignment mismatches against official metadata ([`reports/metrics/manifest_audit.json#L80-L84`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/manifest_audit.json#L80-L84)). For classical tabular models, `SimpleImputer` and `StandardScaler` are wrapped in scikit-learn pipelines and fit strictly on `X_train`, ensuring test and validation features never inform imputation medians or scaling parameters ([`scripts/04_train_baseline.py#L125-L135`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/scripts/04_train_baseline.py#L125-L135), [`scripts/10_train_extra_models.py#L52-L65`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/scripts/10_train_extra_models.py#L52-L65)). For deep learning, log-mel spectrogram normalization statistics in `data/processed/norm_stats.json` were computed exclusively across training audio windows ([`src/audio/spectrogram.py`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/src/audio/spectrogram.py)). These protocols guarantee that no distributional statistics from validation or test tracks leaked into the training representation.

### Q8: What are the exact input features for each model architecture in the repository?
Classical models (Random Forest, AdaBoost, RBF SVC, and ExtraTrees) operate on a 51-dimensional tabular feature vector extracted per track in [`src/features/extraction.py#L24-L43`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/src/features/extraction.py#L24-L43). These 51 features comprise 26 timbral features (13 MFCC means, 13 MFCC stds), 12 tonal features (12 chroma band means), 8 spectral features (centroid, rolloff, bandwidth, and zero-crossing rate means and stds), 2 dynamics features (RMS energy mean and std), and 3 rhythm features (tempo, onset strength mean and std). In contrast, the GenreCNN model takes as input three 10-second log-mel spectrogram windows per 30-second track, shaped $(1, 128, 431)$ with 128 mel frequency bins and an FFT hop length of 512 ([`configs/config.yaml#L38-L42`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/configs/config.yaml#L38-L42)). At test time, GenreCNN averages its softmax predictions across the three 10-second windows to produce a single track-level prediction vector ([`src/models/genre_cnn.py`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/src/models/genre_cnn.py)).

### Q9: How are the log-mel spectrogram normalization statistics computed, and why is this method sound?
Spectrogram normalization statistics are stored in `data/processed/norm_stats.json` and comprise a global mean of -43.32 dB and a standard deviation of 15.19 dB across log-mel power values ([`data/processed/norm_stats.json#L2-L3`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/data/processed/norm_stats.json#L2-L3)). These parameters were computed exclusively across training windows, completely excluding validation and test spectrograms ([`src/audio/spectrogram.py`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/src/audio/spectrogram.py)). During training and inference, each spectrogram window is normalized via standard z-scoring: $x_{\text{norm}} = (x - \mu_{\text{train}}) / \sigma_{\text{train}}$ ([`src/audio/spectrogram.py`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/src/audio/spectrogram.py)). Computing fixed global statistics from training data preserves the relative loudness and spectral energy differences across tracks while ensuring zero data leakage into evaluation splits.

### Q10: Why does the 2D GenreCNN outperform classical models on raw classification accuracy and macro-F1?
GenreCNN achieves 50.62% test accuracy and 0.4970 Macro-F1, outperforming the best classical model (Random Forest at 43.75% accuracy and 0.4354 Macro-F1) by nearly 7 percentage points ([`reports/metrics/final_test.json#L18-L19`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/final_test.json#L18-L19), [`L225-L226`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/final_test.json#L225-L226)). Classical models receive 51 summary statistics that collapse time into static global means and variances, completely discarding temporal cadence, harmonic transitions, and rhythmic motifs ([`src/features/extraction.py`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/src/features/extraction.py)). GenreCNN processes raw $(128 \times 431)$ spectrogram patches using 2D convolutional filters that learn joint time-frequency representations such as harmonic overtones, transient drum onsets, and melodic contours ([`src/models/genre_cnn.py`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/src/models/genre_cnn.py)). Furthermore, GenreCNN benefits from multi-window aggregation, averaging predictions across three distinct 10-second segments of each song to increase prediction robustness ([`configs/config.yaml#L39`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/configs/config.yaml#L39)).

### Q11: How were the classical models (Random Forest, AdaBoost, RBF SVC, and ExtraTrees) tuned?
All classical models were tuned using grid searches evaluated strictly on the official validation split, without cross-validation on test data ([`scripts/04_train_baseline.py`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/scripts/04_train_baseline.py), [`scripts/10_train_extra_models.py`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/scripts/10_train_extra_models.py)). Random Forest searched `n_estimators` in $[200, 400]$ and `max_depth` in $[12, \text{None}]$, selecting 400 trees with unconstrained depth ([`reports/metrics/baseline_val.json#L5-L8`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/baseline_val.json#L5-L8)). AdaBoost searched `n_estimators` in $[50, 100, 200]$ and base tree `max_depth` in $[1, 2, 3]$, selecting 100 estimators and depth 2 ([`reports/metrics/baseline_val.json#L230-L233`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/baseline_val.json#L230-L233)). RBF SVC tuned $C \in [1, 3, 10, 30]$ and $\gamma \in [\text{'scale'}, 0.01, 0.003]$, selecting $C=10, \gamma=0.01$, while ExtraTrees evaluated `max_features` in $[\text{'sqrt'}, 0.3]$, selecting $\text{'sqrt'}$ with 400 trees ([`reports/metrics/extra_models_val.json#L30-L31`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/extra_models_val.json#L30-L31), [`L150-L152`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/extra_models_val.json#L150-L152)).

### Q12: Why do Random Forest, AdaBoost, RBF SVC, and ExtraTrees all cluster at ~43.6%–43.8% test accuracy?
On the held-out test split, Random Forest achieves 43.75%, ExtraTrees achieves 43.62%, and RBF SVC achieves 43.62% accuracy ([`reports/metrics/final_test.json#L18`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/final_test.json#L18), [`L87`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/final_test.json#L87), [`L156`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/final_test.json#L156)). This clustering is consistent with an empirical information capacity ceiling of global spectral summary statistics on this dataset, where temporal sequence dynamics are collapsed into scalar means and variances ([`reports/metrics/baseline_val.json#L260`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/baseline_val.json#L260)). Because summary statistics compress 30 seconds of audio into 51 scalar metrics, critical temporal dynamics, syncopation, and chord voicings are irrecoverably lost regardless of whether a tree ensemble or kernel method is used. Consequently, four distinct tabular classifiers converge to nearly identical test accuracy floors on the 51-dimensional feature set.

### Q13: Why and how does the soft-voting ensemble improve classification performance over individual models?
The soft-voting ensemble combines class probability distributions using validation-optimized weights: 0.40 for RBF SVC, 0.60 for GenreCNN, and 0.00 for Random Forest ([`reports/metrics/final_test.json#L294-L297`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/final_test.json#L294-L297)). It achieves the highest performance on both validation (62.88% accuracy, 0.6198 Macro-F1) and test (52.38% accuracy, 0.5173 Macro-F1), outperforming standalone GenreCNN by +1.76% accuracy on test ([`reports/metrics/final_test.json#L299-L300`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/final_test.json#L299-L300)). The observed test gain is likely attributable to combining orthogonal feature modalities: tabular spectral statistics versus continuous time-frequency spectrogram representations. In ambiguous cases where GenreCNN's spectral convolutions are uncertain, the calibrated margin of the RBF kernel on global features provides a complementary discriminative signal.

### Q14: What is the MERT audio embedding pipeline implemented in the repository, and how does it function?
Implemented in `src/features/embeddings.py` and `scripts/05_extract_embeddings.py`, the pipeline uses the pretrained `MERT-v1-95M` transformer model from Hugging Face (`m-a-p/MERT-v1-95M`). Audio is resampled to 24,000 Hz and divided into six non-overlapping 5.0-second windows per 30-second track ([`scripts/05_extract_embeddings.py#L1-L9`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/scripts/05_extract_embeddings.py#L1-L9)). Each window is embedded into a 768-dimensional latent vector under `torch.inference_mode()`, producing a $(6, 768)$ per-window matrix and a mean-pooled $(768,)$ track-level embedding vector. The extraction pipeline is fully resumable, caching `.npy` embedding files per track with an integrity index to support interruptible batch processing ([`scripts/05_extract_embeddings.py#L7-L8`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/scripts/05_extract_embeddings.py#L7-L8)).

### Q15: Why were pretrained MERT embeddings excluded from the final benchmark results in `reports/metrics/final_test.json`?
Empirical profiling measured an extraction latency of 15.7 seconds per track using 3.0-second overlapping windows, and 8.7–9.4 seconds per track using 5.0-second non-overlapping windows on CPU. Across all 7,994 tracks in the dataset, this translates to an estimated ~19 hours of continuous CPU compute time. Pretrained MERT embeddings were excluded from the final frozen benchmark primarily because of this projected compute duration during development. The extraction pipeline was implemented, validated on pilot tracks, and frozen with unit tests for future transfer-learning experiments ([`tests/test_embeddings.py`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/tests/test_embeddings.py)).

### Q16: How were corrupted or unreadable audio files detected and handled in the data pipeline?
The data preparation script `scripts/01_build_manifest.py` enforces a strict minimum duration threshold of 25.0 seconds of decodable audio ([`configs/config.yaml#L10`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/configs/config.yaml#L10)). Six corrupted MP3 files were identified and cataloged with exact failure causes in `reports/metrics/data_exclusions.csv`: three had truncated audio under 2.0 seconds (tracks 98565, 98567, 98569), and three had corrupted headers with 0.0 seconds of audio (tracks 99134, 108925, 133297). All six excluded tracks originated from the training split, leaving the validation and test splits pristine with exactly 800 tracks each ([`reports/metrics/manifest_audit.json#L17-L21`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/manifest_audit.json#L17-L21)). This filtering reduced the final dataset from 8,000 to 7,994 valid tracks without introducing class imbalance in the evaluation sets ([`reports/metrics/manifest_audit.json#L5-L16`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/manifest_audit.json#L5-L16)).

### Q18: What are the primary technical and acoustic limitations of the SonicFusion 2 system?
The system is constrained to 30-second audio clips, ignoring long-term structural progressions, bridges, and extended movements ([`configs/config.yaml#L37`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/configs/config.yaml#L37)). The training partition contains 6,394 tracks, which is modest for deep learning models, limiting the capacity of GenreCNN to 3 convolutional blocks to avoid catastrophic overfitting ([`reports/metrics/cnn_val.json#L5`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/reports/metrics/cnn_val.json#L5)). Handcrafted tabular models suffer from an information bottleneck by reducing multi-scale temporal signals to 51 global summary metrics ([`src/features/extraction.py`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/src/features/extraction.py)). Finally, genre labels in FMA are single-label ground truths assigned to subjective, culturally fluid musical categories, which penalizes models that predict legitimate hybrid genres [needs author citation].

### Q19: What concrete engineering and modeling directions represent the most impactful future work?
The most immediate enhancement would be running the implemented MERT-v1-95M pipeline across the full dataset to train a classification head on rich 768-dimensional self-supervised audio representations ([`src/features/embeddings.py`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/src/features/embeddings.py)). For the CNN, adopting attention-based architectures such as Conformer or SpecAugment time-frequency masking would likely increase robustness against acoustic variations [needs author citation]. Formulating the task as multi-label genre classification would better reflect musical reality by accommodating tracks that blend elements of Folk, Rock, and Pop simultaneously. Finally, calibrating prediction probabilities via temperature scaling or Platt scaling would improve probability alignment when weighting heterogeneous ensemble components.

### Q20: Why do minor numerical differences appear for certain models across different files in `reports/metrics/`?
Discrepancies exist between isolated historical experiment runs and the final unified benchmarking script, as documented in [`docs/EVALUATION.md#L182-L210`](file:///c:/Users/Yashvie%20Mahey/OneDrive/Desktop/dev/Sonicfusion2/docs/EVALUATION.md#L182-L210). Validation was used for all tuning and ensemble-weight selection; reported test numbers come from `scripts/11_evaluate_final_test.py`; an earlier test run exists in `test_metrics.json`. For example, Random Forest validation accuracy is 52.38% in `baseline_val.json` from the original grid search, but 52.25% in `extra_models_val.json` when the frozen serialized `.joblib` model was re-evaluated in the multi-model comparison. SonicFusion 2 maintains full transparency by preserving historical logs and publishing an explicit reconciliation table rather than retroactively altering generated artifacts.
